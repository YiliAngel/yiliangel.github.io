Overview

A body-worn haptic system that guides blind and low-vision runners through turns and hazards — without relying on audio, which competes with the ambient sound runners depend on. The goal is to let a runner feel where to go, directly on the body.

What I'm Building

  • A belt of distributed skin-stretch actuators that deliver directional cues on the torso, paired with on-device computer vision and a companion iOS app.
  • A novel guidance mechanism I am studying: sequential (cranio-caudal) versus simultaneous cueing — whether pulling in a head-to-toe sequence communicates a turn more clearly than pulling all at once.
  • In-field evaluation with blind and low-vision runners, to test whether the cues actually work at running speed and in real environments.

A manuscript on this work is in preparation.
